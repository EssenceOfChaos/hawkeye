The table `lifecycle_emails` records which email was sent to whom and when; it is deleted with the account.

> **PDF reports and branding (added later; confirm with counsel):** Pro and Agency can download a monthly PDF uptime report per website, and may have last month's reports emailed to the owners of the workspace on the 1st (attached PDFs, from the normal sender; never to the owner's clients). Agency can upload a name and logo that is printed on its reports. New personal data/content: a display name and a logo image per organization (stored in the database, cleaned and re-encoded on upload, deleted when removed or with the account), and a record of which owner was sent which month's email. The Privacy Policy (sections 2A, 3 and 5) and the Terms (section 5) now describe this: reports are informational and "not a guarantee, warranty, or certification" of availability, owners decide whom to forward them to, and the customer may upload only a logo they have the right to use. Open questions for counsel: whether the "not a guarantee" wording is strong enough for a document an agency hands to its own clients (who are not our users), and whether we need an indemnity for logo content. The reports carry a small "Monitoring by HawkEye" attribution in the footer of branded reports (`internal/reportpdf`, `footer`); remove it there if white-label reports are wanted.

> **Free tools (added later; confirm with counsel):** the public SSL certificate checker and website status checker (`/tools`) make our servers connect to a domain a visitor types, from our server's address, and the pages are covered by the same analytics as the rest of the site (events `tool_use`, `tool_result` with the outcome only, `tool_cta_click`; the domain is never sent to analytics). The checkers do not save the domains checked: a domain is held in memory for a few minutes to enforce limits and is logged only at debug level; rate-limit counters are keyed by a hash of the visitor's IP address (purged after 24 hours). The website checker identifies itself as `HawkEye-StatusChecker/1.0` and requests only a site's home page. Confirm the Privacy Policy and Terms cover this (what visitors submit to the tools, the connections we make on their behalf, and that the tools are provided as is, without guarantee that a result is current or complete).

> **Secret manager (added later; mention to counsel):** production settings (payment-provider keys, the database password, the credential-encryption key, the SMTP password, backup bucket keys) are stored in Infisical (`docs/secrets.md`). It holds no customer personal data, so it is not a sub-processor of customer data in the usual sense, but it is a vendor with access to the keys that protect it; list it wherever the policy or a security questionnaire lists vendors that can affect data security.
>
> **Team invitations (added later; confirm with counsel):** an owner on a plan with more than one seat (Agency) can enter a colleague's email address, and HawkEye emails that address one invitation (a link valid for 7 days, only a hash of its token is stored). The invitee need not have an account and has not agreed to anything yet: the address is stored (with the inviting account and the time) until the invitation is accepted, cancelled or expires (expired rows are cleared the next time that organization invites anyone, and all of them are deleted with the organization). It is a one-off transactional message sent at an account holder's request, carries no marketing and no unsubscribe link, and is capped per recipient address (3 per hour by default). Joining shows the organization's owner the person's email address, and a member can see the other members' addresses. The Privacy Policy does not yet mention invitations; open questions for counsel: whether it should list invited-but-not-yet-registered addresses as a category, and whether the invitation email needs a way for the recipient to say "I don't know this person".

## Personal data inventory

| Data | Why | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Email, password hash (Argon2id), name | account | until account deletion |
| Session records (hashed token, expiry; no IP or user agent stored) | sign-in | until expiry/sign-out |
| Monitor URLs/names, check results (status, latency, error class) | the service | per plan: 7 / 90 / 365 days; incidents kept |
| Alert recipient emails | alerts | until removed or account deleted |
| Slack / webhook destination URLs and webhook signing secrets (stored encrypted; only the host is ever displayed) | alerts | until removed or account deleted |
| Maintenance windows (name, schedule, covered monitors) | suppress alerts during planned work | until removed or account deleted |
| Status page (address, title, description, chosen monitor names; **public once published**) | customer-facing status | until unpublished/deleted or account deleted; cached under a minute |
| Billing: Stripe customer ID, subscription state | billing | until account deletion; Stripe keeps its own records (tax/accounting law) |
| Webhook event payload copies | idempotency/debugging | 90/180 days |
| Rate-limit counters (hashed keys) | abuse protection | 24 h |
| Contact-form messages (name, email, message, optional website, account id/plan if signed in) | support | live in the support mailbox, not the database; decide a deletion period |
| Server logs (request ids, no bodies; tokens redacted) | operations | set by your log retention |

Alert content (monitor name and address, event time, short failure description) is also sent to customer-chosen Slack and webhook endpoints; Slack is a third party the customer chooses, not our sub-processor.

Sub-processors to list: hosting provider, database provider, SMTP provider, Stripe, Sentry (if `SENTRY_DSN` set; events are scrubbed of emails/tokens), uptime monitor.

## Decisions for counsel

* Controller/processor roles (customers monitor their own sites, but alert-recipient emails may belong to third parties).
* Lawful bases, DPA for business customers, international transfers, cookie notice (the app sets only a strictly necessary session cookie and CSRF cookie).
* Self-service export (`GET /api/v1/account/export`) and deletion (`POST /api/v1/account/delete`) exist; confirm they satisfy access/erasure obligations, including the shared-organisation case (owners must transfer or delete first) and backups (deleted data persists in backups until they expire: state the period).
* Terms: acceptable use (monitoring only sites you are authorised to test), SLA/no-SLA, liability, refunds/disputes (handled manually today), governing law, trademark check of the "HawkEye" name and logo.
* Stripe Managed Payments / tax registration.
